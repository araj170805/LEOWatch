# Satellite Safety and Operator Response

Satellite safety is the set of practices operators use to protect active
spacecraft from collision with debris or other satellites once a warning
is received.

## Responding to warnings

When conjunction assessment produces a high-risk event (low miss distance
or high probability of collision), operators follow a decision ladder:

1. Improve tracking: request additional observations or updated ephemerides
   to shrink uncertainty before doing anything drastic.
2. Monitor: re-screen as new data arrives; many warnings dissolve when
   refined orbits show the pass is actually wide.
3. Prepare a maneuver: compute a small avoidance burn that changes the miss
   distance while minimizing fuel and disruption to the mission orbit.
4. Execute: typically performed hours before TCA, followed by post-maneuver
   re-screening to confirm the event cleared.

## COLA

COLA (Collision Avoidance) refers both to the overall process and to launch
COLA — screening launch windows against close approaches so rockets do not
fly through congested regions during ascent.

## Maneuver planning basics

Avoidance maneuvers are usually small along-track burns that change the
orbital period slightly so the satellite arrives at the encounter point
early or late by tens of seconds to minutes, multiplying the intrack
separation. Operators must balance fuel cost, mission downtime, and the
creation of new screening burdens for everyone else.

## Screening cadence

Operational conjunction messages arrive roughly three times per day per
event, with decisions often deferred until the final update 6-12 hours
before TCA, when uncertainty has shrunk enough to act confidently.
